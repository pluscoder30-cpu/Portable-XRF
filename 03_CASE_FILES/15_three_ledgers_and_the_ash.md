**The read:** the plant's own tables reproduce its own national
release filings — **the metal flow is measured three ways (Appendix E
water, Appendix F solids, NPRI releases) and every one of the three is
the ceiling frame: "did we stay under the limit."** The floor frame
("what is it worth") appears in none of them.

**The iron gap:** the same report states *"Ferrous chloride consumption
for phosphorus removal totalled 1,549 tonnes as Fe"* in 2024 — the
plant's largest chemical input by mass. **Appendix F has no Iron row.**
The influent Fe load is ~3.3 mg/L × flow ≈ ~545 t/yr; the ADDED iron
(1,549 t) is ~3× the natural load; the Fe-P flocs carry the cake's
metals with them — and the iron itself passes into the 43,418 t of
land-applied cake **unreported in the biosolids analysis.** The
treatment chemical that binds the metals is invisible in the ledger
that measures them.

## 15.2 THE HIGHLAND CREEK ASH ROUTE — where the concentrated stream goes

**Source:** City of Toronto, *Highland Creek Treatment Plant Annual
Reports 2021–2025*; Class EA for Biosolids Management at HCTP (Feb
2016); Health Impact Assessment (Oct 2015); City of Toronto Biosolids
Management page (updated Jun 2026).

| element of the route | the record |
|---|---|
| the plant | HCTP: rated **219,000 m³/day**; serves eastern Toronto (~500,000 people, 15,250 ha); effluent to Lake Ontario; **the only City plant that incinerates all its biosolids** |
| the incinerators | **two 38-year-old multiple-hearth incinerators** (as of 2015); ~10,000 dry t/yr incinerated (26–29 dry t/day); dewatered cake at 26–28% TS |
| the ash | *"This thermal reduction process produces an ash that is mixed with effluent water from the scrubbers and pumped to one of two ash lagoons. When a lagoon is full, ash is removed and hauled to a landfill site for final disposal."* — the **City's own Green Lane Landfill** (per the Class EA and HIA); lagoons cleaned **once per year**; haul routes Beechgrove/Lawrence/Port Union documented in the HIA |
| the ash removals (tonnes/yr, as reported) | 2021: 3,293.3 · 2022: 3,564.0 · 2023: 4,335.6 · **2024: 6,427.5** · 2025: 3,277.3 — *wet-basis ambiguous (ash + scrubber water slurry); the dry ash content is not stated in the reports* |
| the emissions line | *"The emissions are tested annually and are well below the regulatory limits."* (City of Toronto) |
| the future | Class EA alternatives: fluidized-bed incineration (new build, **construction 2029**), haul biosolids off-site, pelletization; the incinerators are at end of service life |
| the city-wide context (2025) | Ashbridges ~40,000 dry t/yr (incl. Humber + North Toronto transfers) + HCTP incinerates ~10,000 dry t/yr; land application, pelletization, chemical stabilization (23% of city's biosolids), incineration, landfill (1% in 2025 during pelletizer repairs) |

**The read:** the incinerated stream is the concentrated stream —
the same metal-bearing solids that Appendix F prices at ~$380k/yr of
measured metals (Cu 672, Zn 670 mg/kg class) go through the
incinerator, lose ~65%+ of their dry mass, and leave the municipal
value ledger entirely: **not in Appendix F (a different plant's
table), not in NPRI (air emissions, not value), not in any asset
line — hauled as ash slurry to a City landfill.** The XRF's reading
at the Green Lane gate would price what the truck delivers; nobody's
ledger has ever priced it.

## 15.3 THE FOOD-CHAIN TRANSFER — the peer-reviewed record (what the
## land application actually does)

**Toronto's 2024:** 43,418 tonnes of cake (Cu 672, Zn 670, Cd ~0.7,
Mn 281 mg/kg dry wt) sent to approved agricultural land application
sites in Ontario. The O.Reg 267/03 frame measures **soil loading**
against maximum acceptable concentrations. The **food-chain transfer**
is the unmeasured path — and it is documented in the literature:

| study | the finding |
|---|---|
| **Bai, Chen, Chang & Page (2010)**, *J. Environ. Sci. Health B* 45(6):531–539 | food plants (cabbage, carrot, lettuce, tomato) grown on biosolids-amended soils **10 years after application**: Cd and Zn in edible tissues **significantly enhanced** vs control soils; Cu, Cr, Ni not significantly increased; uptake of indigenous and biosolids-borne metals **"independent and mathematically additive"**; sandy loam > clayey loam |
| **Barbarick, Ippolito & McDaniel (2015)**, *J. Environ. Qual.* 44(1):286–292 | **the EPA Part 503 uptake-coefficient (UC) framework assumes UCs are constants — they are not**: with up to 10 repeated applications (1993–2013), UC values for Cu, Fe, Mo, Ni, P, Zn **follow exponential decay models (R² 0.329–0.879)**; only the cumulative-removal/cumulative-added ratio is constant (R² 0.471–0.990). *"UC values will likely not provide constants for risk assessment where multiple biosolids applications are made on the same site."* |
| **Barbarick et al. (2016)**, *J. Environ. Qual.* 45(4):1400–1404 | path analyses, same sites 1993–2014: biosolids additions had the greatest positive direct impact on grain Fe concentrations and on P, Zn, Fe, Ni uptake |
| **Sukkariyah, Evanylo, Zelazny & Chaney (2005)**, *J. Environ. Qual.* 34(6):2255–2262 | single high-metal application in **1984**: DTPA-extractable Cu and Zn still **decreased only 58% and 42% by 1984+17 years**; plant tissue metals increased with rate (within normal range); **the metals persist for decades** |
| **ASA/CSSA/SSSA abstract (2015)** | lettuce, zucchini, tomatoes grown 2011–2013 on EQ biosolids at 25 and 50 Mg/ha: **Zn higher in zucchini and tomatoes** vs control; measured concentrations ~54% below safe lifetime concentrations — **the transfer exists; the margins are the safety argument** |
| **Contangelo, Esperschuetz & Robinson (2023)**, *Life* 13(1):1243 | willows on 4% biosolids soil: **foliar Zn 600–1,000 mg/kg — 25× the average NZ pasture**; Cu doubled to 10 mg/kg; *"The land application of biosolids can result in the unacceptable accumulation of TEs in agricultural soil and potentially introduce xenobiotics and pathogens into the food chain."* |
| **Yadav et al. (2025)**, *3 Biotech* 15(11):367 | ten Indian WWTPs: Zn > Cr > Cu > Ni > Pb > Cd > Mo > As > Se in biosolids; wheatgrass uptake: **Cr highest accumulation, then Mo > Zn > Ni**; As, Se, Cd, Pb minimally absorbed; the Immobilization Ratio index proposed to guide application rates |

**The read:** the transfer from land-applied biosolids into edible
plant tissue is **measured, replicated, and persists for 10–17+ years**
(Cd, Zn significant; Zn in fruiting vegetables; foliar Zn 25× pasture
in trees). Toronto's 43,418 t of 2024 cake is the Ontario instance of
the documented path. The regulatory frame (O.Reg 267/03) measures soil
loading against ceilings; **the food-chain transfer of the same metals
is the unmeasured column** — and the EPA's own risk framework rests
on uptake coefficients the data show are not constants (Barbarick
2015). The XRF does not measure transfer; it measures the material at
the gate — the point where the stream could be priced before it enters
the food chain.
