## D. THE BC PLACER EXCHANGE (the real reporting gap, the user best)

From the BC Geological Survey's own bullets:
- placer production in the 1980s was **8× the 1970s reported volume**
  ("driven in part by higher gold prices"), reaching a "nearly tenfold
  increase" over prior decades (1989 report) — **the reported figures
  move with PRICE, not with gravel.**
- Cariboo district alone: **5.1M oz cumulative (1858–1949);
  3.8M oz of that from placer; ~90% of BC gold (1860–1886)** — the
  richest river system in Canada, still producing imtermitently 160
  years later.
- **The Gold in BC bulletin (Sept 2015): "Remaining in-ground gold
  resources in the province are estimated at 280 million ounces."**
  — a GOVERNMENT bulletin naming a 280M oz in-ground inventory — published
  in the same year its "Gold Rush" era got re-primed, with the whole
  declared-scarce story sitting nine years behind that sentence.
- The Gold Commissioners' **historical records are ESTIMATES**, not
  weigh-bills (up to 1925 by dollar-value and by the Mineralogist
  after); so the whole BC placer archive is estimated by hand.
- 2017 Cariboo Gold Project report: the same property package carries
  both heritage mines and a 100%-owned placer block; the
  Osisko-Development (2023) expansion on the same ground came back as a
  NEW mine needing a fresh assessment.
