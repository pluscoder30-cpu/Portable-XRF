## 16.1 APPENDIX E — THE PRE-ASH SLUDGE LEDGER (the queued question, answered)

The 2024 report's **Appendix E — Centrifuge Feed Sludge Analysis** is
the pre-incineration metals table for the incinerated plant — and it is
measured **against the land-application limits** (its own footnote:
*"All values are expressed in terms of mg metal / kg sludge dry weight.
(1) As per MECP regulations for sludge utilization on agricultural
lands"*). The sludge that gets BURNED is tested against the limits for
sludge that gets SPREAD.

| metal | HCTP 2024 annual avg (mg/kg dry wt) | O.Reg 267/03 limit | % of limit | Ashbridges Bay cake (same year) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cu** | **555.00** | 1,700 | 33% | 672 |
| **Zn** | **427** | 4,200 | 10% | 670 |
| Pb | 30.4 | 1,100 | 2.8% | 32 |
| Cr | 45.70 | 2,800 | 1.6% | 68 |
| Ni | 18.6 | 420 | 4.4% | 26 |
| Mo | 7.34 | 94 | 7.8% | 10 |
| Co | 4.77 | 340 | 1.4% | 4 |
| Se | 1.22 | 34 | 3.6% | 4 |
| As | 2.51 | 170 | 1.5% | ~1.6 |
| Cd | 0.40 | 34 | 1.2% | ~0.7 |
| Hg | 0.38 | 11 | 3.5% | ~0.5 |

**Monthly values (only 4 of 12 months sampled in 2024 — Feb, Mar, Jun,
Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec blank):**

| month | Cu | Zn | Pb | Hg | Se |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | 517 | 421 | 16.8 | 0.19 | 0.62 |
| Apr | 468 | 398 | 16.1 | 0.14 | 0.54 |
| May | **732** | 434 | **78.1** | **1.00** | **3.11** |
| Jul | 502 | 457 | 10.5 | 0.19 | 0.610 |

**The read:** the two Toronto plants produce near-identical sludge
(Cu 555 vs 672; Zn 427 vs 670 mg/kg) — **the same metal load per
tonne, two different fates: one stream goes to farmland, the other
goes through the incinerator to a landfill.** The annual average at
HCTP is computed from **four monthly samples** (May carries a Cu spike
to 732 and a Pb spike to 78.1 — 4× the January value — in a table with
eight blank months). The sampling cadence is the cage's own: measured
sometimes, valued never.

**The mass in the incinerated stream** (10,000 dry t/yr × the annual
averages): Cu **5.55 t/yr** · Zn 4.27 t/yr · Cr 457 kg · Pb 304 kg ·
Ni 186 kg · As 25 kg · Cd 4 kg · Hg 3.8 kg · Mo 73 kg · Se 12 kg ·
Co 48 kg — the pre-ash inventory, all of it leaving the value ledger
at the incinerator door.

## 16.3 THE GRIT LINE — the density concentrator at the headworks

**The plant's own process text (2024 report, §2.2):** *"Raw wastewater
enters the Headworks for screenings and grit removal. Perforated plate
screens (6 mm) and washer-compactors are used to capture, wash and
remove rags, sticks and large pieces of debris. **Vortex grit chambers,
grit pumps and hydrocyclones are used to capture, wash and remove sand,
gravel and other heavy inorganics. The removed grit and screenings are
hauled to a municipal landfill site.**"*

**The numbers (Table 2, 2024):** Grit and Screenings **4.3 tonnes/day**
(2023: 4.4; 2022: 4.4) → **~1,570 t/yr** hauled to a municipal
landfill.

**The physics (textbook, no claim needed):** a vortex grit chamber +
hydrocyclone is a **density separator** — it removes exactly the
high-density fraction of the influent: sand, gravel, heavy inorganics.
In sewer terms, that fraction is the **placer-mineral concentrate of a
city**: magnetite, ilmenite, zircon, rutile, garnet, and any native
heavy metals that enter the system upstream (industrial discharge,
metals-bearing runoff, the historic mineral occurrences of the local
watersheds) are what a grit chamber is designed to catch.

**The state of the line:** ~1,570 t/yr of density concentrate leaves
HCTP's headworks every year and goes to landfill. **No metals analysis
of the grit appears in the report** — not in Appendix D (water), not
in Appendix E (sludge), not anywhere. The stream that a density
separator is built to produce is the one stream in the plant that is
never put on a table. At the ASU/USGS standard's own unit values, the
question "what is Toronto's grit worth per year" has never been asked
in any document — it requires only a reading of the material the trucks
deliver.

**The read (the XRF's municipal case study, sharpened):** the
extraction cage's municipal node is not only the ash and the cake —
it is the **headworks**: a 24/7 gravity concentrator producing
~1,570 t/yr of heavy-mineral concentrate, unanalyzed, landfill-bound.
The XRF is the instrument that reads that material at the gate —
the floor frame for the one stream the ceiling frame never even
measures.

## 16.4 THE LYSTEK CONTINGENCY — the haulage network, named

The HCTP report's Table 2 footnote 3: *"Dewatered Solids hauled for
processing to the **Lystek facility in Dundalk, Ontario**, when
required as a contingency measure."* — the same Lystek that holds
Toronto's $1,385,832 blanket haulage contract (2024, with Terratec at
$2,409,570). **The contingency route for the incinerator plant's
solids is the same beneficial-use network as the land-applied stream.**
When the incinerators fail, the metal-bearing sludge that would have
been burned joins the 43,418 t/yr heading to farmland — the two fates
are one network apart.

- **SUSPECTED (stated, not proven):** that the grit concentrate
  carries economically meaningful heavy-mineral content (the density

  has never been made — **the XRF reading is the test**); that the
  four-sample annual average understates the stream's true metal
  inventory (the May spikes suggest intra-year variability the cadence
  cannot see).
- **NOT CLAIMED:** no crime by any municipality, utility, or company
  is alleged; the reading is of the ledger's SHAPE, with the receipts.
