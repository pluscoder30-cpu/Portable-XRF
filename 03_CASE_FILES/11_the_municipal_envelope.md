## 11.1 THE INPUTS (every number sourced, 2026-11-03)

### The per-ton value (the ASU/Westerhoff standard)
- **US$280 per ton of sludge** — the 13 most lucrative elements (Ag, Cu,
  Au, P, Fe, Pd, Mn, Zn, Ir, Al, Cd, Ti, Ga, Cr) at 2014–15 prices;
  **$480 per dry ton** in the EST follow-up (Westerhoff et al. 2015,
  *Environ. Sci. Technol.*): "For a community of 1 million people,
  elements in sewage sludges were valued at US $13 million annually
  ($480/dry ton)" — 20% of the value ($2.6M/yr) is gold and silver.
- **USGS (Kathleen Smith)**: "the gold we found was at the level of a
  minimal mineral deposit… might be commercially viable to mine it."
  16,500 US WWTPs → **~7 million dry tons/yr** biosolids nationwide.
- **Eawag/Swiss FOEN (2017)**: CHF 3M/yr gold+silver lost through
  Swiss effluent and sludge (population ~8.5M); Ticino concentrations
  "sufficiently high for recovery to be potentially worthwhile."

## 11.2 THE ARITHMETIC (the multiplication nobody's ledger did)

**Method:** municipality's own dry-tonnage × the ASU per-ton value
($280/ton sludge or $480/dry ton) = the annual metal mass-value the
stream carries. This is a **public-data multiplication**: every input
is a sourced number above; only the multiplication is ours. It is not
a claim about what is extractable — it is a claim about what the
stream IS, priced at the standard's own unit value.

| municipality | dry tonnes/yr (own filings) | × $280/ton (ASU sludge) | × $480/dry ton (EST follow-up) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Toronto** (Ashbridges Bay + transfers) | ~40,000 | **$11.2M/yr** | **$19.2M/yr** |
| **Toronto Highland Creek** (incinerated, metals concentrated by >65% dry-mass loss*) | ~10,000 | — (concentrated; per-ton value ↑) | — |
| **DC Water** (Blue Plains; ~147k wet t @ ~26% TS ≈ 38k dry t) | ~38,200 | **$10.7M/yr** | **$18.3M/yr** |
| **Metro Vancouver** (60k bulk + 35.6k managed) | ~95,600 | **$26.8M/yr** | **$45.9M/yr** |
| **US total** (USGS: 7M dry t/yr × 16,500 plants) | 7,000,000 | **$1.96B/yr** | **$3.36B/yr** |

\* incineration concentrates metals by >65% dry-mass reduction (PMC 2021:
"For nearly all reported metals, incineration increases the metals
concentration by up to one order of magnitude") — the ash per-ton value
is HIGHER than sludge; the 10k dry t of Toronto's incinerated stream
carries more metal value per tonne than the 40k dry t of land-applied
stream.

**Gold+silver alone (the Eawag rate, scaled):** Switzerland CHF 3M/yr
Au+Ag for ~8.5M people → CHF 0.35/person/yr → Toronto (6.2M) ≈
**CHF 2.2M ≈ US$2.5M/yr in gold and silver alone**, from one city's
sewers, at one reference's own per-capita rate.

**The DC Water comparison (their own numbers):** Bloom fertilizer
revenue **$13.6M/yr** — the ORGANIC value. The metal value of the same
stream (ASU's own $280/ton × their own tonnage) ≈ **$10.7M/yr**. The
ledger books the compost; the metals pass through unbooked. DC Water
publishes the monthly metals table (Cr/Cu/Mo/Ni/Se/Zn in mg/dry kg)
in their own reports — the data exists; nobody has multiplied it.

**The Toronto contracts (the haulage layer):** Lystek $1.39M +
Terratec $2.41M = **$3.8M/yr to move the stream** — the cost of
moving the mass is booked; the value of the mass is not.

## 11.3 THE PRECEDENT THAT PROVES THE PATTERN (someone already tried)

**Werribee, Australia (1995):** *Echidna Mining* — an Australian gold
exploration company — **acquired the exploration rights to the ground
surrounding Melbourne's historic sewage sludge stockpiles** (1898–1980,
large closed lagoonal tanks) and "commenced a program of resource
evaluation" of the sludge as a gold resource. Published in *Hydrometallurgy*
(Reeves, Plimer & Foster, 1999): *"Sewage sludges… may be considered
targets for economic extraction of Au because of the documented Au
content of sewage sludges worldwide which are of the order of some ore
deposits currently mined for Au."* — the paper cites Furr et al. (1976)
American cities' sewage gold contents.

**The read:** in 1995, a mining company looked at a city's sewage and
called it a gold deposit. The paper is in the literature; the company
acquired the rights; the evaluation ran. **The pattern is not
hypothetical — it has been executed**, in Australia, thirty years ago,
by a company whose name is in the journal. What happened next (whether
Echidna mined it, whether it was declared, whether the rights lapsed)
is the case's next question — but the FACT that a gold exploration
company treated Melbourne's sewage as an ore body is on the record.

## 11.4 THE TRUTH POLICY (kept)

- **CONFIRMED (sourced above):** every tonnage is the municipality's
  own filing; the per-ton value is the ASU/USGS/Eawag standard; the
  arithmetic is public; the DC Water metals table exists in their own
  monthly reports; Toronto's haulage contracts are City Council
  documents; the Echidna Mining precedent is a published paper.
- **SUSPECTED (stated, not proven):** the pattern is consistent with
  a system where the metal value of municipal streams is measured
  (16,000 tests/yr in Metro Vancouver; monthly tables in DC Water;
  Appendix F in Toronto) but never multiplied into a ledger line, while
  the organic/fertilizer value IS booked ($13.6M Bloom; $3.8M Toronto
  haulage). The asymmetry — organic booked, metal unbooked — is the
  cage's municipal signature.
- **NOT CLAIMED:** no crime by any municipality, utility, or company
  is alleged; the reading is of the ledger's SHAPE, with the receipts.
