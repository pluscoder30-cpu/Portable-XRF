## 17.2 DC WATER — THE SECOND MUNICIPALITY, THE SAME SHAPE

**Sources:** DC Water Biosolids Resource Recovery Monthly Reports
(2016); Blue Plains EPA tour deck (2023); DC Water Board/EQO Committee
packages (2023–2026); Green Bond Report FY2024; PEER Independent
Engineering Assessment (2023).

**The production (their own numbers):**

| line | the record |
|---|---|
| design capacity | **Class A biosolids 120–160 dry t/day, 380–500 wet t/day, 28–34% TS** (EPA tour deck) → **~44,000–58,000 dry t/yr** |
| monthly actuals | Jan 2024: 12,462 t produced, 8,979 t land-applied, 3,483 t Bloom sold (18,630 t FY24 cumulative); Dec 2025: 12,439 t produced, 6,321 t land-applied, 6,118 t Bloom sold; May 2023: 14,124 t produced, **9,684 t sold (record)** |
| Bloom sales goals | FY23 58,000 t · FY24 65,000 t · FY26 62,000 t — **the fertilizer line is a goal, tracked monthly, in the board package** |
| reuse cost | $38.82–$39.98/wet ton (2016) — the cost of moving the mass, booked monthly |
| the metal table (Oct 2016 report, mg/dry kg) | **Cr 67 · Cu 434 · Mo 15.6 · Ni 24.0 · Se 4.45 · Zn 775** + the Centrum comparison (Zn 1,279%, Cu 305% of Blue Plains levels) |
| the storage network | Cumberland County pad (~25,000 t), Cedarville lagoon (~30,000 t), Goochland pad, Fauquier lagoon (~15,000 t) — **and March 2016: "staff began sending biosolids to a Waste Management landfill in VA for use as daily cover"** |
| the corporate frame | Blue Drop = DC Water's nonprofit LLC (2016) marketing Bloom + HQO Waterside Events; 100% of biosolids recycled (FY2024 Green Bond); "all biosolids met the EPA's Exceptional Quality requirements" |
| the grit line | the tour deck's process diagram: **"Screening & Grit Removal 225 mgd"** — the same density-separator stage as Toronto's vortex chambers, capacity-rated, never priced |

**The two-frame arithmetic (same method as Toronto):**

| frame | the number |
|---|---|
| **the ceiling frame (what the filings do)** | "below the EPA limits," "considerably below the national average," "Exceptional Quality," Centrum comparison — **the metals are contaminants** |
| **the floor frame (the multiplication)** | ~44,800 dry t/yr (mid-range of design and monthly actuals) × the October 2016 table: **Cu 19.4 t ($181k) · Zn 34.7 t ($94k) · Cr 3.0 t ($24k) · Ni 1.1 t ($18k) · Mo 0.70 t ($15k) · Se 0.20 t ($10k) ≈ $342k/yr of measured metals** |
| **the ASU standard on the same tonnage** | 44,800 t × $280/t = **$12.5M/yr** — the 13-element value of the stream, at the national standard's own unit value |
| **what is booked** | Bloom fertilizer revenue ($13.6M cited in 2026 reporting); the reuse cost ($39.98/wet t); the storage-pad capacities |
| **what is unbooked** | the ~$342k of measured metals; the ~$12.5M standard-value envelope; the 225 mgd grit stream, unanalyzed |

**The read:** DC Water runs the same two frames as Toronto — the
ceiling frame in every board package ("below the limits," the Centrum
vitamin comparison), the floor frame in none. The fertilizer revenue
is a monthly goal in the board deck; the metal value of the same
material is a multiplication nobody has performed. The grit stream
(225 mgd capacity) sits beside it, unanalyzed — **the national
pattern: the density concentrator at the headworks, the concentrated
stream at the back, and the value column missing from both.**
