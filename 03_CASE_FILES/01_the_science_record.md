# THE EXTRACTION CAGE — 01 — THE SCIENCE RECORD
## The "they knew" ledger: every public discovery the cage stood next to
## Sources are live-checked; each number carries its URL

The pattern of the case: **measure → announce → measure again → decline →
call it waste.** The concealing gesture is always the same sentence —
*"recovery would not be economically worthwhile"* — issued by an
institution, never an operator's plan of record. What follows is the
chronology of the world being TOLD the truth about abundance while the

## 2007 — THE JOINT RESEARCH (the quiet discovery year)
- **Nagano Prefecture + Japan Sewage Works Agency** jointly found that
  **incinerated sewage sludge ash carries gold at a concentration
  "comparable to that of a high-grade ore."**
- **The gate sentence:** *"because the cost of extracting the gold
  outweighed the potential profit, the operator continued treating the
  ash as an industrial waste material."*
  Both quoted by NextNature/Climateer citing Japanese press (Yomiuri,
  Nagano Nippo). **Anyone could have read this in 2009.**

## 2008–2009 — THE PUBLIC RUN (a city proves it works)
- **Urban: Suwa, Nagano Prefecture — Nagano Prefecture Suwa Construction
  Office** (a government operating agency): 1.89–1.9 kg gold per tonne of
  molten fly ash, from 100,000 t/day wastewater → ~3 t ash/day. First
  month earned ¥5M (~$56k). Reuters ran it as a novelty ("more gold-rich
  than the ore of the Hishikari Mine, Japan's major goldmine, which
  averages ~20 g Au/t").
- **Amsterdam MSWI bottom-ash science** (Springer, Dec 2008): 2–6 mm
  non-ferrous concentrates carry gold pieces; the **MDS (magnetic density
  separation)** process is "simple and economically feasible" for
  300,000 t/yr incinerators.

## 2010 — THE INVENTORY (Japan's own numbers)
- **NIMS (National Institute for Materials Science, Mar 26 2008 → the
  estimate published 2010): ~6,800 tonnes of gold recoverable in Japan's
  used electronics alone.**
- **Dr. Harada + Tohoku University (RtoS research committee, est. 2006)**
  proposed "artificial ore recycling" — the "urban concentrates" concept
  with an IMPORTANT design: **not direct extraction; CONCENTRATION first.**
  That's the same logic our chain runs: measure → concentrate → refine.
- Wikipedia/Wikipedia mirror: "The name was coined in the 1980s by
  Prof. Hideo Nanjyo, Research Institute of Mineral Dressing and
  Metallurgy, Tohoku University."
- **2010 rare-earth squeeze** (China cut REE exports to Japan — the
  Senkaku Incident): after this, "urban mining" got hard Japanese
  government funding streams (JOGMEC; by 2026 a ¥37.9B national program).

## 2011–2012 — THE SEDIMENT WINDOW (public papers, exactly the
## user's predicted window)
- **Tsurumi River (Japan, publ. Mar 2011):** urban sediment trace metals
  3–4× background; the process breakdown (sequential extraction) shows
  33–80% of Zn/Pb/Cd/Co in anthropogenic binding.
- **Niitsu River (Japan, publ. Aug 2012):** 12 metals mapped across
  urban/agricultural segments; Al Cr Fe Zn Cd Pb significantly elevated;
  mobile/altered fractions named per metal.
- **That same window opens the financial signature** … see 04: the
  impairment avalanche (2012–2013, the $4.4B/$10.4B Barrick years), the
  2013 KPMG "BC Junior Mining at a Crossroads" (write-offs continue,
  "investors may see mining companies in the red — all participants
  agreed this is healthy") — **the industry said its own red books are
  healthy.** The weekly cadence of "yearly losses" stopped being news.

## 2014 — THE DAM-SLUICING RECORD (the water-work engines)
- **IHA sediment-management case-study library** opens public: Dashidaira
  (Japan, Kurobe cascade): annual drawdown + sluicing + flushing since
  1994; sediment pulses at **13,000–48,000 mg/L**; the estimate
  9×10⁴ m³/event vs actual flushed **"much greater than estimated values"**
  (their own Figure 7). Cachí (Costa Rica): trap efficiency changed
  **82% → 27%** by flushing. Teesta V (India): FOUR flushing events/year.
  Kali Gandaki (Nepal): desanders + seasonal sluicing.
- **Kondolf et al. 2014 (Earth's Future):** "Trapping of sediment by dams
  is not inevitable… a range of proven techniques." (The technological
  option existed and is named in the open literature.)
- **2014 — Arase Dam REMOVAL** (Kuma River, Japan): dam gates opened April
  2010 → sediment cores 2011–2013 → heavy-metal enrichment DECREASED
  downstream as the impoundment stopped trapping. (Dam operations CONTROL
  where the enrichment sits — the engineering is real.)

## 2015 — THE AMERICAN STUDY (the number the whole press ran)
- **Westerhoff et al., Arizona State University, Environmental Science &
  Technology / Science 16 Jan 2015:** **a 1-million-person city's annual
  sludge carries ~$13M of metals, $2.6M in gold+silver; 13 lucrative
  elements at $280/ton of sludge** (Ag Cu Au P Fe Pd Mn Zn Ir Al Cd Ti Ga
  Cr). USGS **Kathleen Smith** (ACS Denver, Mar 2015) seconded; reported
  next steps: "pinpoint sources and determine how to extract."
- **The gate sentence at work:** ***"Although no U.S. sewage plants have
  followed suit."*** Same finding in Yale's **Peccia** quote ("we need to
  stop thinking of it as a liability…") — the academic cover phrases it as
  aspiration rather than policy demand.
- **The barrier voice:** **Charles Bott, R&D chief, Hampton Roads
  Sanitation District (1.7M served):** "Cost is one of the chief barriers
  keeping sewage treatment plants from going for the gold." While
  HRSD was already extracting phosphorus/nitrogen (i.e., selling
  nutrients) — the metal line stayed off the ledger.
- **DC Water (the D.C. precedent visible in the same window):** the
  **Bailey Bioenergy Facility** ($470M, commissioned Oct 2015, first
  thermal-hydrolysis in North America) + **Bloom** biosolids compost
  business — "$13.6M generated" (Axios, Jul 2026). **DC Water plans
  monetize the organic matter and NEVER the metals**, even though ASU's
  paper (from their own repositories) quantified the metals in the same
  year the facility opened.

## 2017 — THE NATIONAL ASSESSMENT (Switzerland public-records the loss)
- **Eawag / Swiss FOEN study (Oct 10 2017), 64 WWTPs:** ~**43 kg gold +
  ~3,000 kg silver lost per year via effluent and sludge (CHF ~3M/yr)**;
  **"sufficiently high for recovery to be potentially worthwhile" at
  Ticino sites** (where the gold refineries sit) and ruthenium/rhodium
  elevated in the Jura (the watch industry). **The national sentence:**
  *"recovery would not be economically worthwhile"* — issued by the same
  agencies whose 2012 ban on using sludge as fertilizer routed every
  Switzerland tonne into incineration with **91% of phosphorus
  recoverable** per FOEN (SwissPhosphor, still coordinating in 2025 —
  the same "study → coordination platform" decade).
- **Bloomberg's contextual line:** ~70% of the world's gold passes through
  Swiss refineries yearly. The refinery hub's own sewage carries
  ore-grade gold. Nobody assays it into the books.

## 2020–2022 — THE FURNACE-FLOOR REVELATION (Japan's
## second announcement)
- **Sagamihara Minami WtE (Japan gov-on-line Oct 2022):** after two years
  of surveys, the operator found the gold concentrated **in the sand at
  the bottom of the fluidized-bed furnace: >6,000 g/t** — 100–1,000× the
  3–5 g/t of mined ore. **15 kg gold + 15 kg silver in FY2021 from ONE
  plant.** "operations… suspended once every 3–4 months" for extraction.
  **The embed sentence:** *"until now there was no way to recover it as
  a resource."* Japan's WTE fleet: 17 such furnaces, recovery started at
  5. The same article: the technology cuts CO₂ per recovered gram
  to <1/10 of ore-refining CO₂.
- **NIES (Japan, Mar 2019):** 4M tonnes of MSW incinerator ash/year in
  Japan; research on density-separation recovery of precious + hazardous
  metals; "the possibility of total recycling."

## 2024–2025 — THE CANADIAN TAILINGS TELLING (the modern report)
- **Action Canada / Public Policy Forum ("Waste Not", March 2025):**
  Canada's mine tailings hold multi-billion-dollar untapped critical
  minerals; **"Canada still lacks a comprehensive national database on
  mine tailings"**; **"companies aiming to re-mine tailings often face
  regulatory frameworks designed for new mining projects"** — the permit
  node, named by a public-policy fellowship, then filed.
- **AER/AGS Information Series 155 (Alberta Geological Survey, 2025):**
  **34,000–39,000 tonnes of VANADIUM concentrated per year** through
  oil-sands processing (2017 production basis); vanadium enriched in
  petcoke, petcoke fly-ash, froth-treatment tailings; TiO₂ 11.5% +
  ZrO₂ 3.40% in the same tailings; Syncrude+Suncor tailings carry about
  **6% of the world's TiO₂ production and 9% of ZrO₂**. The bulletin's
  "needs research" note sits under a pile that had **>8 years of R&D and
  $80M+ (Titanium Corp) without commercialization** — the cage's own
  research cadence.
- **2013–2026 DC Water** (continued): "Bloom generated $13.6M" — the
  organic value walks out the gate; the metal value of the same material
  (quantified by the same ASU study published the year after the
  facility's commissioning) never appears in a DC revenue line.
