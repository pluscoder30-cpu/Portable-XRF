## 10.1 THE AER TMR MASS LEDGER — the regulator tracks mass, not metals

The Alberta Energy Regulator's annual Tailings Management Reports
(Directive 085 / OSCA conditions) are the densest public mass ledgers
on the continent — and they carry NO metals column.

| facility / year | the tracked mass (from the TMR itself) | the un-tracked metals (from AER/AGS INF 155) |
|---|---|---|
| **Syncrude Mildred Lake 2025** (Approval 8573U, submitted Apr 2026) | fines in ore processed **25.6 Mt**; rejects 1.4 Mt; AN froth 2.2 Mt; new FT 10.7 Mt; Sankey diagrams of fines distribution; site-wide water balance tables (in/out by node) | **V 34–39 kt/yr** through the same flow; TiO₂ 11.5% + ZrO₂ 3.40% in froth-treatment tailings — absent from the TMR |
| **Suncor Fort Hills 2025** | ore processed **112.1 Mt**; average composition: bitumen 10.6%, mineral 83.4%, water 6.1%, **fines 24.5%**; fines in FT 2024 = 35.5 Mt → 2025 = 42.4 Mt; mass balance via "instrumentation + laboratory analysis of samples" | the same vanadium/Ti/Zr concentrations (AER/AGS 2025) |
| **Syncrude Aurora North 2024/2025** (Approval 10781Q/S) | fines in ore 26.7 → 28.8 Mt; new FT 9.7 → 12.7 Mt; water balance by node (recycle 103.0 Mm³, losses 29.0 Mm³ pore water) | ditto |
| **AER State of FT Management 2024/2025** | all operators below approved profiles; volumetric audits; RTR criteria (65% solids in 1 yr for CT) | no metals column anywhere in the AER's own annual |

**The read:** the regulator's chain measures **water and mass with
instrument-grade precision** (Sankey diagrams, node-by-node water
balances, annual volumetric audits with uncertainty statements) — and
the same filings' subject (per AER/AGS Information Series 155, Feb 2025)
carries **34,000–39,000 tonnes of vanadium per year** and the
world's second-richest titanium resource. The mass ledger is complete;
**the value ledger has never been opened.** The XRF reads what the AER
already counts — and adds the column the regulator never wrote.

## 10.6 THE ASSAY-CAP PRACTICE — the top-cut, declared

Canarc's own MD&A: *"assays were capped at 75.0 g/t for Au and 700.0
g/t for Ag"* in the El Compas resource estimation — **the top-cut
(top-cutting / capping) is a standard, declared statistical practice**
in resource estimation (it limits the influence of high-grade outliers
on the block model). It is not hidden; it is disclosed in the MD&A.
**The read:** the cap is legitimate practice — and it is the same
*shape* as the price-impairment: a declared adjustment to the number
that governs the declared value. The XRF does not cap; it reads the
line as the physics returns it. The contrast is the point: the cage
adjusts; the instrument reports.
