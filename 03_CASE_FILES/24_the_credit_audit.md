## PART 2 — SITE C: "saying no to the upfront extraction, but still extraction"

The user's instinct: the flood extracts too — new sediment downriver,
a permanent waste stream. The physics receipted is an **inversion of
the sediment part** and a **confirmation of the waste-stream part** —
the stream is mercury, and it travels downstream.

### The reservoir as a sediment machine (receipted)

- **The reservoir TRAPS ~70%** of its own tributary and shoreline
  sediment inputs; the suspended load **leaving the dam drops 54%**,
  fading to **−2% by the Smoky River confluence (283 km downstream)**
  (Knight Piésold, ICOLD 2017). Our outflux is **98% clay** (baseline
  river: 37% clay / 55% silt / 8% sand). Sand and most silt stay.
- **The dam MANUFACTURES its own sediment stream:** shoreline erosion
  delivers **1.1 million t/yr of fines in Year 1, falling to 0.55 Mt/yr
  by Year 10** — 57% of all suspended inputs (KP 2017). It deposits
  **throughout the reservoir** (0.3–0.5 m thickness over most of it in
  50 years; the 15-km Halfway River embayment infills in **150–220
  years** — the reservoir's own death clock).
- Downstream (Church 2014; Carver 2012 review): bedload **ceased** in
  the regulated cobble-gravel reach; clear-water starvation
  re-engineers the river — **aggradation at tributary confluences, a
  stepped long profile** — and Peace River residents report the change
  **with their own hands**: *"there appears to be an increase in silt
  along the shore … like clay … slippery and difficult to traverse"*
  (Mighty Peace Watershed Alliance, 2023). So the user's "higher
  tailings downriver" is the **right detection, inverted mechanism**:
  less sediment goes down, but a **new, never-existing sediment body**
  (the reservoir's own erosion + trapped load) is created and held, and
  the river's banks and bars downstream are re-cut by clear water.
- **None of this mass was ever assayed for metal content, and none of
  it is in a financial ledger.** The EIS measured metals in *water*
  (compliance frame). The floor frame — element mg/kg × tonnage ×
  spot on the trapped/eroded/deposited sediment — **does not exist.**
  Same gap as Toronto's ash, the same gap as the AER's 25.6 Mt of
  fines (file 10).

### The waste stream that actually travels: methylmercury (receipted — BC Hydro's own program)

- Mechanism (their words): *"When soils are flooded, degradation of
  the organic material creates favorable conditions for
  sulfate-reducing bacteria that transform or 'methylate' some of the
  inorganic Hg into … methylmercury (MeHg)"* (EIS fact sheet).
- Prediction: reservoir fish **3–4× baseline**, peaking **5–8 years
  after fill**, normalizing over **20–30 years**; **downstream fish
  ≈2× baseline as far as Many Islands, Alberta (~120 km)** (MMP).
- Exposure framing (their documents): a child under 12 eating **one
  serving per month of a 22-inch bull trout** is **~10% of the
  methylmercury pTDI at baseline — ~40% at predicted peak** (Azimuth,
  2025).
- The program exists (annually for 10 years, then every 5, until
  "stable"), built with the Indigenous Nations. The reservoir filled
  **August–November 2024**; the project hit full operation in **2025**;
  **2025 reservoir sampling was skipped for floating-debris safety**
  (MMP 2024 draft). **The mercury ledger is kept as monitoring — it
  exists in no financial ledger as a cost.** The power books as
  revenue. **The flooded-land waste stream is the floor frame's
  perfect absence: perpetually measured, never booked.** (First
  post-fill reservoir fish results: **UNBOUND — queued.**)
- Credit boundary: the defenders' fights are receipted (Boones, Tupper,
  Stewart Phillip, the camps; the injunctions against **every** group
  dismissed; BC Hydro's own injunction granted Feb 29, 2016, S-160488).
  No Nation ever operated or joined the dam. **Their "no" was real,
  lost at Site C itself —** and their credit stands.

### The correction the register needed (Blueberry)

Our Site C row under-sold the defenders. **Yahey v BC, 2021 BCSC 1287
(June 29, 2021, Justice Emily Burke)** is the **first Canadian ruling
that cumulative effects of many projects can constitute treaty
infringement**: *"the Province may not continue to authorize activities
that breach the promises included in Treaty 8"* — four declarations.
**Blueberry River won.** Then: initial agreement (Oct 7, 2021: $65M;
195 pre-decision projects proceed, 20 do not), and the **Implementation
Agreement (Jan 18, 2023)**: **$200M restoration fund by June 2025;
$87.5M/3-year package; >650,000 ha protected from new forestry/PNG;
~50% cut in new PNG disturbance; old-forest and trapline
protections** — **and** *"a series of timber harvesting and oil and
gas activities to proceed"* (the same shape again: **the win became a
contract, the contract continues extraction under negotiated caps** —
identical geometry to Voisey's Bay's halt→IBA; The agreement's own
words).

### The symmetry (the register's newest pattern line)

Both corridors' biggest fights ended as **contracts, not stoppages**:
the Innu's halt → the IBA (2002); Blueberry's court win → the
Implementation Agreement (2023). The Nation-level record stays clean:
**no receipted Nation operates extraction in either corridor.** The
extraction continues under Vendors (Vale) and the Crown (BC Hydro) —
with the waste streams (the mine's tailings, the reservoir's sediment
and mercury) **measured by their own programs and missing from every
financial ledger.** The only Innu-branded vehicle inside the
extraction chain is the **86835/Rich Resources exploration shell** —
a private company in the loss-runner's orbit, NOT a Nation act — and
that shell is already on the register as row A-13, now person-bound
to a former Innu Nation VP.
