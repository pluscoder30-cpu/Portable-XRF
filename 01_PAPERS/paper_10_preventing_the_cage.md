## Abstract

The extraction cage persists because the measurement is optional,
the ledgers use only the compliance frame, the courts enforce the
process and not the mass, and the ethics law contains no sanction.
We propose six interventions — **instrument-mandated floor-frame
disclosure, the waste-as-asset accounting line, the two-way assay
gate, standing for the mass, teeth for the audit, and the municipal
floor-frame publication** — none of which punish anyone: they
require only that the value the physics already sees be written into
the ledgers that already exist. The XRF (government-graded, 10/10
REAL, plant-gate cost) makes the mandate feasible; the floor frame
(element mg/kg × tonnes × spot) is the arithmetic the filings already
contain. Prevention here is not prosecution — it is **measurement
that the structure was built to exclude.**

## 1. The diagnosis: why the cage persists

Four structural facts (all receipted in files 01–22):

1. **The measurement is optional.** No law requires the waste
   stream to be assayed for value. The compliance frame measures
   safety (the ECA limits, the O.Reg 267/03 ceilings, the EPA 503
   EQ limits) — never worth. The labs assay what is submitted; the
   waste is never submitted.
2. **The ledgers use only the ceiling frame.** Toronto books the
   nutrient phosphorus and the $3.8M haulage; the $10.7M envelope is
   unbooked. DC Water's Bloom line hits its goal every year at
   $5.98/ton; the metals at $280/ton are unbooked (47×, per ton).
   Kobelco's ¥68–73B segment sales carry no metals column. The AER's
   TMRs track 25.6 Mt of fines and never write "vanadium."
3. **The courts enforce the process, not the mass.** Site C's
   challenges were all dismissed; BC Hydro's injunction was granted;
   WOLV's FFCTOs enforce the filings, not the properties; Barrick's
   class action alleges the price, never the mass. **No court has
   ever been asked what the tailings, the ash, the grit, or the
   sewage are worth.**
4. **The ethics law has no sanction.** SDTC: $336M in conflicted
   funding decisions, 82% of transactions with conflicted directors
   — and the Ethics Commissioner's own words: *"the Act does not
   provide for any sanctions."* The entire enforcement apparatus is
   a report that goes to a committee.

The cage is structural: no one is breaking a law; **the law was
never written to see the waste.**

## 2. Proposal 1 — Instrument-mandated floor-frame disclosure

**The rule:** every entity that operates a waste stream above a
tonnage threshold (tailings, ash, grit, sludge, furnace sand, dredge
spoil) must publish, annually, the **floor-frame line**: element
mg/kg (measured) × tonnes (weighed) × spot price (published) = the
stream's contained value, for every element above the instrument's
detection limit. The compliance frame (the ceilings) stays; the
floor frame is added beside it.

**Why it is feasible now:** the XRF reads the stream at plant-gate
cost with a government-graded instrument (GOV battery 10/10 REAL:
SRM 2783 CRM gate, EPA ±20% replicate bands, ISO 13528 scores, the
F3 proof's 11.27 orders of EM-to-mass margin). The encapsulated
streams (the furnace sand's 20 μm gold in 1 mm sand) are read
whole-sample with the matrix correction — the engine has the model;
the fire assay's total-pulp-metallics approach is the reference
standard. **The measurement exists; only the mandate is missing.**

**What it does not do:** it does not require extraction, recovery,
or profit. It requires only that the number already in the material
be written into the ledger that already exists. The company that
books $5.98/ton of fertilizer would book, beside it, the $280/ton
the standard says the stream carries — and the gap becomes visible
in the filing, not in a research paper.

## 3. Proposal 2 — The waste-as-asset accounting line

**The rule:** the metal value of waste streams (the contained
elements at spot) becomes a **disclosed line item** in the financial
statements — an asset at the measured value, or a disclosed
non-asset with the reason stated. The IAS/GAAP extension: waste with
a measured contained value above a threshold is inventory, not
expense.

**The precedent in their own records:** Kobelco sold ~30 kg of
recovered gold for ~¥37M (June 2022) — a completed transaction,
cash received — and booked it in no segment. The accounting standard
would have required the line. The Hudbay AIF already contains the
contradiction (100 Mt of tailings beside "a reprocessing
opportunity"); the standard would require the value to be stated
with the tonnage.

## 4. Proposal 3 — The two-way assay gate

**The rule:** the assay spine's accreditation (ISO 17025) extends
to the waste streams adjacent to the lab's clients. If a lab assays
a mine's ore, it must offer (and disclose whether it performed) the
same suite on the mine's tailings. If a lab assays a plant's output,
it must offer the suite on the plant's grit and ash. **The submission
layer becomes a two-way gate: what goes in, comes back out.**

**Why the spine can carry it:** the labs already have the methods
(AAS, fire assay, ICP-MS, the ultra-trace suites), the QC stack
(blanks, duplicates, CRMs), and the round-robins (Canmet, Rocklabs).
Eastern Analytical's two-lab pattern (local fire assay + BV
Vancouver's 59-element ultra-trace) is the model: the waste suite
rides the same chain. The cost is marginal; the disclosure is the
change.

## 5. Proposal 4 — Standing for the mass

**The rule:** environmental proceedings gain a cause of action for
the **unbooked mass** — the difference between the stream's measured
contained value (the floor frame) and the value booked in the
ledgers. The plaintiff need not prove crime; they must prove the
measurement (the instrument, the tonnage, the price) and the absence
of the line. **The injunction register's missing question becomes
justiciable: what is the waste worth, and why is it not in the
ledger?**

**The precedent:** the Innu's 1995 halt (the only injunction that
stopped extraction in the corridor) was won on rights, not mass —
and resolved by a contract (the IBA condition), not a ruling on the
value. Site C's defenders argued consultation and treaty rights; no
one argued the mass. The class actions argue the price (Barrick's
$3B offering after the disclosure); no one argues the tailings.
**Standing for the mass closes the register's longest gap.**

## 6. Proposal 5 — Teeth for the audit

**The rule:** where an Auditor General finds conflicted funding
decisions (the SDTC pattern: $76M conflict-connected, $336M
total, 82% of transactions), the finding becomes an **order** —
recovery, disqualification, or a public registry entry — not a
report to a committee. The Conflict of Interest and Ethics
Commissioner's reports gain a sanction schedule (the current Act:
*"does not provide for any sanctions"*).

**Why it matters to this case:** the public money (SDTC's $684M;
the Junior Exploration Assistance grants) flows to the extraction
industry's wastewater treatment (Swirltex/ARC, Saltworks), the
biosolids stream itself (Xogen's "eliminates biosolids"), and the
mining equipment layer (Motion Metrics) — through boards whose
conflicts the AG measured at 82%. **The audit found the flow; the
law cannot stop it. Teeth make the finding a wall instead of a
paper.**

## 7. Proposal 6 — The municipal floor-frame publication

**The rule:** municipalities publish, beside the compliance reports
(the ECA tables, the NPRI releases, the metals charts), the
**floor-frame multiplication**: their own tonnage × their own
measured concentrations × spot = the stream's contained value. The
data already exists (Toronto's Appendix E/F; DC Water's "electronic
version only" monthly metals; Metro Vancouver's 16,000 tests/yr);
the multiplication is the only step missing — and it is arithmetic.

**The instance:** Toronto's own waste-hierarchy diagram contains a
"metal recovery and recycling" step; the ash skips it. The
publication would make the skip visible in the City's own document.
DC Water's board deck tracks Bloom volume as a monthly goal; the
floor-frame line would sit beside it.

The instrument is the enabling condition for all six proposals:

## 9. What this is not

These proposals punish no one. They do not allege crime; they do not
seize assets; they do not stop extraction. They require only that
the value the physics already sees — the 6,000 g/t sand, the 11.5%
TiO₂ tailings, the Cu 672 cake, the 1,570 t/yr grit, the ¥100M
gold — be written into the ledgers that already exist, measured by
an instrument that already passes the government's own battery. **The
prevention is measurement, not prosecution.** The truth policy holds:
no crime is claimed beyond what the filings and courts state; the
reading is of the structure's SHAPE; every absence is spoken.

## 10. Conclusion

The extraction cage is a measurement gap wearing the clothes of an
industry. Four structural facts keep it in place: optional
measurement, ceiling-only ledgers, process-only courts, sanction-less
ethics law. Six interventions close it — floor-frame disclosure,
the waste-as-asset line, the two-way assay gate, standing for the
mass, teeth for the audit, the municipal publication — each no more
than a line in a filing that already exists, each enabled by an
instrument that already passes the battery. **The cage was built by
omission; it falls by the same mechanism: the number, written down.**
