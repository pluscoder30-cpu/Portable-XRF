## Abstract

We describe the physical processes by which the extraction cage's
waste streams concentrate value — mining (density separation,
froth treatment), municipal water treatment (grit chambers, digestion,
incineration), sewage processing (capture, dewatering, land
application), and waste-to-energy (fluidized-bed gasification,
melting) — and show that in every process the density fraction (the
high-specific-gravity concentrate) is the value stream, that the
measurement chain (the assay spine) stops at the plant gate, and that
the ledgers that follow use only the compliance frame. The gap is
structural: the labs do exactly what they are paid to do (assay the
submitted sample); the waste never enters the paper chain; the courts
enforce the process, not the mass; the ethics law contains no
sanction. We present the three-stream municipal case study (grit →
cake → ash) as the canonical instance, the furnace sand (6,000 g/t,
20 μm gold inside 1 mm sand grains) as the process-physics proof, and
the AER's own tonnage ledgers (tonnes tracked, metals absent) as the
regulator's version of the same gap. The cage is the shape of the
pipeline, not a conspiracy — and the XRF is the instrument that reads
the stream the pipeline discards.

## 1. The thesis: the submission layer

Every extraction process has a **submission layer** — the point where
material enters the paper chain (the assay, the invoice, the
regulatory report). The paper chain carries what the process outputs;
it does not carry what the process discards. The assay spine (AGAT,
SGS, Bureau Veritas, Inspectorate, Eastern Analytical, the Syncrude
Research Lab, the SRK QPs) measures the submitted sample to ISO 17025
with round-robins and umpires. **The cage is the submission layer:**
the labs are not the problem; the fact that the waste stream never
reaches them is.

## 2. The processes, one by one

### 2.1 Mining — the density concentrate (files 09, 10, 17)

The mine's waste (tailings) carries the density fraction the plant
did not float: the AER's own report (INF 155, Feb 2025) puts
**TiO₂ 11.5%, ZrO₂ 3.40%** in froth-treatment tailings and
**34,000–39,000 t/yr of vanadium** through the same flow — "in the
tailings," never "in the lab." The TMRs (Mildred Lake: 25.6 Mt
fines; Fort Hills: 112.1 Mt ore, 42.4 Mt fluid tailings) track the
mass at instrument-grade precision with **no metals column.** The
process physics: froth treatment separates bitumen from solids; the
solids (the fines) concentrate the heavy minerals; the tailings
ponds hold the concentrate; the ledgers hold the tonnes.

**The Voisey's Bay instance:** the Innu's 1995 eviction → the court
halt of advanced exploration → the IBA contract term — the only
injunction that ever stopped extraction in the corridor, resolved by
a contract, not a ruling on the mass. The deposit moved to Inco
($525M for 30%), then Vale ($4.3B for 75%); the land-claim question
stayed open; the Innu-owned junior now stakes claims in the same
territory with provincial grants.

### 2.2 Municipal water treatment — the grit chamber as a density concentrator (files 15, 16, 20)

A vortex grit chamber + hydrocyclone is a **density separator** —
it removes exactly the high-density fraction of the influent: sand,
gravel, heavy inorganics. In sewer terms, that fraction is the
placer-mineral concentrate of a city: magnetite, ilmenite, zircon,
rutile, garnet, and any native heavy metals entering upstream. The
numbers: **Toronto Highland Creek: 4.3 t/day ≈ 1,570 t/yr** of
"sand, gravel and other heavy inorganics," hauled to municipal
landfill, **no metals analysis in the report.** **DC Blue Plains:
337 t in one month ≈ 4,000 t/yr** (their EQ Ops package), unanalyzed.
**The process runs 24/7; the value column was never written.**

The same plants' solids: Toronto's Appendix F (Cu 672, Zn 670 mg/kg
dry wt) and HCTP's Appendix E (Cu 555, Zn 427) — the two Toronto
plants produce near-identical sludge; **one stream goes to farmland
(43,418 t in 2024), the other goes through the incinerator (~10,000
dry t/yr) to a landfill (the ~45,000 t ash series).** The incinerator
concentrates by >65% dry-mass loss; the ash is never analyzed; the
City's own waste-hierarchy diagram contains a "metal recovery and
recycling" step that the ash skips.

The chemical-iron line: Toronto adds **1,549 t/yr of ferrous
chloride as Fe**; Appendix F has no Iron row; the treatment chemical
that binds the metals passes into the cake unreported. HCTP's effluent
iron (0.8508 → 0.879 mg/L) exceeds its influent — the chemical
visible in the water column, invisible in the solids table.

### 2.3 Sewage processing — the capture and the transfer (files 11, 13, 15)

The sewer captures the city's metals; the plant concentrates them in
the solids; the solids go to land. The three ledgers of one plant
cross-check: Toronto's Appendix E (water) × flow ≈ the plant's own
NPRI filings (Pb ~411 kg ≈ 500–622 reported; As ~821 ≈ 699–1,200;
Cd ~329 ≈ 115–482) — **the metal flow is measured three ways and
every way is the ceiling frame.** The peer-reviewed transfer record:
Cd and Zn in edible tissues significantly enhanced 10 years after
application (Bai et al. 2010); the EPA's uptake coefficients are not
constants (Barbarick et al. 2015 — exponential decay, R² 0.329–0.879);
Cu and Zn still 58%/42% extractable 17 years later (Sukkariyah et al.
2005); foliar Zn 25× pasture in trees (Contangelo et al. 2023). The
transfer is measured, replicated, persistent — and unbooked.

The precedent: **Echidna Mining (1995)** acquired Melbourne's 1898–1980
sludge rights and ran a 31-element, 5-method study; Reeves, Plimer &
Foster (1999): *"Sewage sludges … may be considered targets for
economic extraction of Au."* The paper is in the literature; the
rights' fate is SPOKEN; the lead's ACN binding is REFUTED (the name
matched, the entity did not).

### 2.4 Waste-to-energy — the fluidized bed as the final concentrator (files 12, 20, 22)

The Kobelco fluidized-bed gasification and melting furnace: waste is
gasified in circulating sand at 500–550°C; the melting furnace melts
the ash at ~1,200°C → vitrified slag (road material). **The gold and
silver concentrate in the sand at the bottom of the fluidized bed** —
discovered in 2018 after a two-year investigation (*"we had no idea
where the gold and silver were"*); **6,000 g/t** in the sand (vs
their own 3–5 g/t ore comparison); **20 μm gold particles locked
inside 1 mm sand grains** (the encapsulation — a surface reading
under-reads; the matrix correction computes the transmission); FY2021:
15 kg Au + 15 kg Ag; **June 2022: ~30 kg sold for ~¥37M profit**;
recovery at **5 of 17** domestic furnaces; the fleet: **19 orders
(17 domestic + 2 overseas)**; the CO2 claim: **<1/10 ore refining** —
the company argues the waste IS ore, in a government publication,
while the P&L books it as waste treatment (three columns, no metals
line).

**The landfill-mining extension:** Sennan (¥19B, 15-year DBO)
processes excavated waste from the final disposal site; Kofu treats
landfill sludge; Nishikawagawa: 10% excavated-waste co-processing
extends the landfill's life from FY2017 to **FY2043 (29 years)**.
**The fleet's business model includes digging up old landfills — the
legacy concentrate (pre-regulation ash, historical sludge) is the
highest-grade target, never assayed.**

### 2.5 Dredging and placer — the gravels (file 10)

The BC placer record: **83 oz from 1,400 cu yd ($47.42/cu yd) at
91% purity** (Noble Metal Group, 2010–11 — operator-disclosed);
Cariboo's 3.83M oz combined (International Wayside); the government's
own sentence: **280 million oz in-ground, Canada.** The grades
(1.74–4.0 g/t; 0.75 g/t tailings reserves) are above the engine's
floor; the operators published them; the ledgers booked the
extraction, not the mass.

## 3. Where the measurement stops

The assay spine's geometry (files 09, 18):

```
[the extractor] → [the submitted sample] → [the lab: ISO 17025, round-robins]
      ↓ certifies grades → [the QP: SRK/GRE: "similar to 2012"]
      ↓ → [the books: price-driven impairments]
[the waste] → [NEVER SUBMITTED] → [tailings / ash / grit / sewage / sand]
      ↓ → [the ledgers: tonnes, no metals column]
```

The labs do their job. The submission layer is where the scarcity
story is made, not the assay. The measurement gap is structural:
**no law requires the waste stream to be assayed for value; the
compliance frame measures safety (the ceilings), never worth (the
floors).**

## 4. The ledgers that follow

At every scale, the same split: **the ceiling frame is funded,
published, and enforced; the floor frame exists nowhere.** Toronto
books the nutrient phosphorus and the $3.8M haulage; the $10.7M
envelope is unbooked. DC Water's Bloom line hits its goal every year
at **$5.98/ton**; the metals at **$280/ton** are unbooked (47×, per
ton, same material). Kobelco's ¥68–73B segment sales carry no metals
column; the ¥100M gold is an IR slide. The AER's TMRs track 25.6 Mt
of fines and never write "vanadium." The courts enforce the process
(Site C's dismissals; WOLV's FFCTOs; Barrick's price claims); no
court has ever been asked what the tailings are worth.

## 7. Conclusion

The cage is the shape of the extraction pipeline: density concentrates
value; the measurement stops at the plant gate; the ledgers carry
the compliance frame and never the value frame; the courts enforce
the process and never the mass. It is structural, not criminal —
which is exactly why it persists: **no one is breaking a law; the
law was never written to see the waste.** The XRF is the instrument
that makes the waste visible at plant-gate cost — and the first
reading entered into evidence is the floor frame the structure was
built to exclude.
