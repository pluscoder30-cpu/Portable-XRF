## Abstract

Historiography usually frames alchemy's fate as a clean replacement story: chemistry succeeded it. The record says something sharper. For roughly sixteen centuries the alchemical tradition documented a doctrine with four load-bearing elements — gold born of the base and meanest matter; the base matter named *in writing*, in a published 1612 dictionary of more than fifty synonyms, as "Urine," "Dung," and "Dissolved Refuse"; the human body as the vessel of the work (the microcosm); and a distinction held inside the tradition between philosophical and literal gold. This paper documents, date-by-date and source-by-source, (1) the doctrine's carrier chain (Zosimos c. 300 AD → the Hermetica → Ruland 1612 → Maier 1617 → the Rosicrucian corpus → Paracelsian spagyry → Newton's c. 1680 Emerald Tablet translation, Keynes MS 28); (2) its three-stage displacement — legal (alchemy banned in England under severe penalties), institutional (Boyle's *Sceptical Chymist* 1661 redefining the theory of matter so the body-doctrine ceased to be chemistry; the Royal Society 1660 institutionalizing the replacement), and archival (Pellet's 1727 "unfit to publish"; the Portsmouth collection locked ~200 years; Sotheby's 1936, 329 lots, over a third alchemical; Keynes's "the last of the magicians"); and (3) the return symmetry — Dobbs's 1975 rediscovery scholarship and the 1979 gold-from-sewage patent family arriving in the same decade, followed by Werribee's 1995 mining evaluation and Suwa's 2009 smelter sale. The physics the papers of this series present explains what the doctrine's holders could not: the reading. What this paper claims is the history — every date checkable against its source.

## 1. The Doctrine, Documented (1490–1660 window and earlier)

### 1.1 The carrier chain

**Zosimos of Panopolis (fl. c. 300 AD)**, the earliest historically attested alchemist, defined the *magnum opus* as "the composition of waters, movement, growth, embodying and disembodying, **drawing the spirits from bodies and bonding the spirits within bodies**" — the tradition's first documented statement that the operation runs on bodies. The sun analogy survives in his fragments: copper that "takes the color of gold, through purification, becomes a terrestrial sun" [VERIFIED — surviving fragments].

**The Hermetica and the Emerald Tablet** carried the doctrine under parable — the tablet's documented reading in the register's baselines: gold-from-base-matter as the *magnum opus*'s frame.

**Martin Ruland the Younger, *Lexicon alchemiae* (1612)** — a published dictionary, not coded marginalia — lists **more than fifty synonyms for the prima materia**, documented verbatim including **"Urine," "Dung," "Dissolved Refuse,"** "Spittle of the Moon," "Venom," "Heart of the Sun," "Indian Gold" [VERIFIED — Ruland 1612]. A dictionary is the one genre that cannot be charged with esoteric ambiguity: the doctrine's base matter is *named in print* as the meanest bodily and refuse substances.

**Michael Maier, *Atalanta Fugiens* (1617), Emblem 36:** the stone "is cast upon the Earth, exalted on Mountains, resides in the Air, and is nourished in the Waters" — found in the low and mean places [VERIFIED — Branwin trans.].

**Thomas Vaughan** ("Eugenius Philalethes"): "the first matter of the stone is the very same with the first matter of all things" [VERIFIED].

**Paracelsian spagyry (16th c.):** *spao* + *ageiro* — to separate and recombine a body's *tria prima*; performed on plants, minerals, and, in doctrine, the human body as microcosm. Paracelsianism is documented as declining "rapidly in the later 17th century with the rise of the scientific movement" — the displacement *dated by the record itself* [VERIFIED].

**The Rosicrucian corpus (1614–17):** the manifestos document "the nine stages of the involutive-evolutive transmutation of the **threefold body** of the human being, the threefold soul and the threefold spirit" [VERIFIED]. **Thomas Browne, *Religio Medici* (1643):** the philosophers' stone is "something more than the perfect exaltation of gold" — the tradition's internal distinction between philosophical and literal gold, held by a physician [VERIFIED].

The tradition to that point: **a coherent, documented, centuries-long theory that gold — or its perfection — works out of the body and its refuse.** What the doctrine never had was the physics of why the body would hold gold at all.

## 2. The Erasure, Date-by-Date

### 2.1 The three-stage mechanism [VERIFIED pattern]

### 2.2 The carrier's overwrite

**Newton translated the Emerald Tablet c. 1680** (Keynes MS 28, King's College Cambridge) and wrote more on alchemy than on physics — documented across the Keynes collection [VERIFIED]. *Principia* (1687) published the ratio-physics Euclid-style — the physics survived, its carrier's name no longer writable. From 1738 forward the "clockwork Newton" is built by Voltaire, Euler, Laplace — with the documented judgment that "Newtonian science sprang from Laplace's work, not Newton's" — the reputational overwrite of the knowledge-holder while his doctrine sat sealed [VERIFIED — docs/22].

**Sotheby's auction, 1936:** the Portsmouth Papers sell — **329 lots, over a third alchemical**; John Maynard Keynes buys heavily and in the Newton Tercentenary celebrations names Newton "**the last of the magicians**," a title presented as the *discovery* of what Newton's own century had hanged men over [VERIFIED]. **1975:** Betty Jo Teeter Dobbs, *The Foundations of Newton's Alchemy, or the Hunting of the Greene Lyon* — the suppressed corpus becomes public scholarship; the transcription projects (Newton Project; Chymistry of Newton Project) put the manuscripts online [VERIFIED].
